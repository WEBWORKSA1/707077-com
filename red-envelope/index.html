Red Envelope Amount Guide 🧧

The amount says as much as the gift. Pick the occasion and your relationship — we suggest lucky totals and flag numbers to avoid.

The unwritten rules

  • Never include a 4. 40, 400 or 444 sound like death.
  • Even amounts for happy events. Weddings favour pairs; 88, 168, 520, 888 and 1314 are popular.
  • Use new notes. Crisp bills show respect and a fresh start.
  • Funerals are different. Condolence money goes in a white envelope, often in odd amounts — never red.
  • Digital hongbao count. WeChat and Alipay red packets follow the same number logic; 520 and 1314 transfers spike on love days.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I give at a Chinese wedding?

It depends on closeness and local cost of living. Friends often give amounts like 520, 666 or 888 in the local currency; close family give more, often 1314, 1888 or 6666. Always avoid 4.

How much is a red envelope for kids at Chinese New Year?

Commonly small lucky amounts such as 88, 168 or 188 in the local currency, more for close relatives.

Can I give an odd amount?

For happy occasions, even amounts are preferred. 9 is an exception sometimes used for longevity (99, 999).
