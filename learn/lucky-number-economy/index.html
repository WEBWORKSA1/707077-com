The Lucky-Number Economy: What Chinese Buyers Pay for 8s

By 707077 Editorial · Updated 1 October 2026

Superstition has a price tag. In Chinese-speaking markets, lucky digits measurably raise what people pay for licence plates, phone numbers, apartments and gifts.

Licence plates: millions for two digits

In 2016, Hong Kong's licence plate 28 sold for HK$18.1 million (about US$2.3 million); in Cantonese, 28 sounds like “easy prosperity”. Plates 18 and 9 had earlier sold for roughly US$2.1 million and US$1.7 million (Bloomberg).

Measuring the 8 premium

A University of Virginia economics study of 1,067 Hong Kong plates sold in seven auctions between May 2024 and April 2025 found that replacing one neutral digit with an 8 raised the expected price by 64.8%. Combinations carried extra premiums: 28 (+42.9%), 68 (+42.3%) and 88 (+37.4%). The 4 effect was not statistically significant, likely because few unlucky plates make it to auction.

Phone numbers

Carriers and resellers in mainland China and Hong Kong routinely price numbers by their digits. Numbers ending in 8888 or containing 168 or 518 are sold as premium inventory, while numbers with many 4s are often cheaper or hard to sell. NBC News reported a buyer paying about US$215,000 for a lucky phone number.

Love days drive spending

  • Ahead of Qixi 2025, one platform reported pre-sale flower orders up 132% year on year, with 11-, 33- and 52-stem bouquets all showing triple-digit growth (Global Times).
  • Restaurant searches for Qixi rose 240%, and set menus priced at 588 to 1,314 yuan were common.
  • A 2023 survey on 520 found over 90% of respondents willing to buy gifts; China's gift economy was projected at ¥1.38 trillion in 2024 (China Trading Desk).

What it means for you

  • Selling to Chinese-speaking customers? Price at 88, 168 or 888; avoid 4s.
  • Buying a number? Lucky digits cost more, but they can also resell for more.
  • Gifting? The amount carries a message: 520, 1314, 888.

Sources: Bloomberg (22 Feb 2016); Hu, “How much would you pay for lucky numbers?”, University of Virginia Economics (2025); NBC News; Global Times (Aug 2025); China Trading Desk (2023).
