Chinese Lucky and Unlucky Numbers: The Complete Guide

By 707077 Editorial · Updated 1 October 2026

In Chinese culture, a number's luck comes mostly from its sound. 8 sounds like “prosper”, 4 like “death”. Here is every digit, the key combinations and how people actually use them.

The lucky numbers

  • 8 (八 bā) — sounds like 发 (fā), to prosper. The Beijing Olympics opened at 8 pm on 8/8/2008 for this reason.
  • 6 (六 liù) — sounds like 溜/流, smooth flowing. 66 = 六六大顺, everything goes smoothly.
  • 9 (九 jiǔ) — sounds like 久, long-lasting. Popular for weddings and anniversaries.
  • 2 (二 èr) — good things come in pairs (好事成双). In Cantonese it sounds like “easy”.
  • 3 (三 sān) — sounds like 生 (life, birth) in Cantonese.

The unlucky numbers

  • 4 (四 sì) — sounds like 死 (sǐ), death. Many buildings skip 4th, 14th and 24th floors; phone numbers with 4s are often cheaper.
  • 14, 514, 74, 748 — read as “want to die”, “I want to die”, “angry to death”, “go and die”.
  • 250 — 二百五 means “idiot”. Avoid as a price or gift amount.

The mixed ones

7 is romantic (亲, kiss; 起, rise) and anchors Qixi, but the 7th lunar month is Ghost Month. 5 reads as 我 (me) in codes but also 无 (without). 0 reads as 你 (you) or as wholeness.

Frequently asked questions

What is the luckiest number in China?

8, because 八 (bā) sounds like 发 (fā), to prosper or get rich.

Why is 4 unlucky in Chinese?

四 (sì) sounds almost identical to 死 (sǐ), death. The belief is strong enough that many buildings skip floors containing 4.

Is 9 lucky in Chinese?

Yes. 九 (jiǔ) sounds like 久, long-lasting, so 9 is linked to longevity and enduring love.
