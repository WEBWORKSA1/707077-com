The Number 7 in Chinese Culture: Kiss, Rise — and Ghosts

By 707077 Editorial · Updated 1 October 2026

Seven is one of the most double-edged numbers in Chinese culture: it means kisses and rising fortunes, but also mourning and ghosts.

The bright side of 七

  • Sound: 七 (qī) echoes 起 (qǐ, rise), 气 (qì, life force), 齐 (qí, together) and 亲 (qīn, kiss or dear). Some also hear 妻 (qī, wife).
  • Love: Qixi (七夕), the festival of lovers, falls on the 7th day of the 7th month. Codes like 770 (kiss you) and 721 (darling) start with 7.
  • Togetherness: 七 sounds like 齐, “complete, together”.

The shadow side

  • Ghost Month: the 7th lunar month hosts the Ghost Festival (中元节), when spirits are said to roam. Many people avoid weddings, moves and big purchases in that month.
  • Mourning: 头七 (tóu qī) is the seventh day after a death, and traditional mourning runs in 7-day cycles up to 49 days.
  • Deceit: 七 can also echo 欺 (qī, to cheat).
  • Cantonese: 七 (cat1) has a vulgar slang homophone in Cantonese, so brands targeting Hong Kong or Guangdong often handle 7 with care.
  • History: 七七 also names the Marco Polo Bridge Incident (7 July 1937).

The verdict

For love and relationships, 7 is positive. For business numbers aimed at Cantonese speakers, or for anything timed during Ghost Month, many people prefer to avoid it. On the lucky checker, 7 is neutral by default and positive in “love” mode.

Frequently asked questions

Is 7 lucky or unlucky in China?

Both. It is romantic and linked to rising energy and Qixi, but the 7th lunar month is Ghost Month and 7-day cycles are part of mourning customs.

What does 7 mean in Chinese texting?

Usually 亲 (qīn), kiss or dear: 770 = kiss you, 775 = kiss me, 721 = darling.
